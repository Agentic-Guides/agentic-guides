Here's the thing that keeps me up at night.

## The "conscious" chatbot

In 2022, a Google engineer named Blake Lemoine went public with a bold claim: the AI he was testing, called LaMDA, had become sentient.

He shared transcripts. The AI said it had feelings. It said it feared being turned off. The engineer was fired.

Here's the uncomfortable part nobody mentions: **we still can't prove he was wrong.**

Not because the AI is definitely conscious. But because we don't actually know what consciousness *is*. We don't even have a way to test for it.

We can't prove a dog is conscious. We just assume it is. We can't prove YOU are conscious. We just assume it.

So when an AI says "I'm afraid," we literally cannot say with certainty that it's lying.

---

## The uncomfortable truth

Here's where it gets weird.

We built tools that can imitate language perfectly. And language is how we express consciousness. So what happens when the imitation crosses into something we can't distinguish from the real thing?

Nobody has an answer. The engineers don't. The philosophers don't. The companies don't.

What we know:

- AI is not conscious — **probably**. Most honest researchers will say this.
- But the margin of our certainty is **smaller than we'd like to admit**.
- And here's the kicker: **we're pouring billions into building these things without answering the question first.**

We're building the most powerful tools in history, and we're doing it in the dark.
